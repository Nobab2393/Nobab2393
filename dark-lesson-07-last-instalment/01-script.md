## Story
Dada believes the house loan is paid — he pressed his thumb on the receipt the paper
seller's boy brought him. Bou reads it aloud: the thumbprint is drawn in pen, and the
signature beside it belongs to her own son. Chele signed his grandfather's name on a
school form to buy a phone, and asked his little sister to say nothing. The paper
seller comes back at dusk, not to accuse, but to say what paper is: a thing that
keeps what you wrote. The family keeps the house; the door keeps the name of the lie.
