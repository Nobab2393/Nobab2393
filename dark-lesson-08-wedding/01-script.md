## Story
A wedding the bride did not choose, paid for by a loan nobody can repay. The shopkeeper
has sent the rice and the oil and has not been paid; the elder signed as guarantor and
believes the bride's family will pay after the wedding; the moneylender holds the deed
with the elder's thumbprint on it and gives them until tonight. Then the postman brings
the groom's letter: they know, and they want the bride's side to pay everything, or the
boy will not come to look. The widow — who watched this bride's mother sell her own
gold on her wedding day so the neighbours would not say the girl went empty-handed —
speaks the one line that matters. The shopkeeper tears up his bill. The moneylender
nails the deed to the wall instead of taking it down. The elder calls the wedding off
and keeps the girl's mother's name written.

## One hour, one light
Everything happens in the same late afternoon. The light never changes between shots;
only the camera moves, and the people do. `grade()` is a no-op on purpose — the palette
is the light.
