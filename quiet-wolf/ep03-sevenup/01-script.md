## VO — 6 segments (~1,270 chars ≈ 105 s)

**S1 — ACT 1 HOOK (colour, diner)**
The pig lifts the bottle and grins. "Seven-Up. Fresh, clean, lemon-lime — takes the ouch out of grouch." The wolf sets down his glass. Son, that bottle was born in 1929 with lithium inside — the same metal doctors would use for bipolar disorder twenty years later.

**S2 — ACT 2 ORIGIN (sepia, 1929 St. Louis)**
1929, St. Louis, Missouri. Charles Leiper Grigg, a former advertising man, mixes lemon-lime syrup at a small workbench. He names it "Bib-Label Lithiated Lemon-Lime Soda" — so long it barely fits the bottle — and sells it as a hangover cure.

**S3 — ACT 2b THE NUMBER (sepia, ad art)**
Why seven? Nobody really knows. Seven ingredients, seven ounces, or the atomic number of lithium itself. Every answer was invented after the name, not before it.

**S4 — ACT 3 MARKETING TRICK (colour poster / sepia ad)**
Then the trick. He shortens the name, sells a feeling, and prints one line on every advertisement: takes the ouch out of grouch. By the nineteen-forties it was the third best-selling soft drink in America.

**S5 — ACT 4 TRUTH (3 science lines, sepia lab)**
Here is the truth, in three lines. One: lithium citrate is a real drug, a mood stabiliser. Two: a soft drink is the worst possible dose — too weak to help, too constant to be safe. Three: in 1949 the government forced lithium out of soft drinks. Seven-Up dropped it and kept the name.

**S6 — ACT 5 LESSON + CTA (colour, eyes glow orange)**
So read the label, not the slogan. The calm was never in the bottle — it was in the marketing. Quiet. Watch. Learn. Subscribe. Quietly, Wolf.

## Fact check before render
- ✅ 1929, St. Louis, Charles Leiper Grigg, original name "Bib-Label Lithiated Lemon-Lime Soda", lithium citrate, marketed as hangover cure / mood lift.
- ✅ Slogan lineage: "takes the ouch out of grouch" (1930s).
- ✅ Lithium removed from soft drinks around 1949–1950 (US ban on lithium in beer/soft drinks after 1949 salt-substitute deaths).
- ⚠️ "7 = atomic number of lithium" is folklore, not documented — script deliberately says nobody knows, and lists it as one invented theory.
- ⚠️ "Third best-selling soft drink by the 1940s" — widely repeated in soda histories; keep as "by the forties" rather than a precise date.
