# Claude Animation Style

The look that made "Claude animations" a format is **not** a video model. Claude writes a small
program that draws the animation, and the program is rendered to MP4. That single fact produces the
whole aesthetic: simple flat shapes (they're cheap to draw in code), perfect easing curves, motion
that is deterministic and re-renderable, and typography that sits still while shapes move around it.

**One rule above all others:** every frame must be a pure function of `t`. No `Math.random`,
no timers, no CSS transitions, no state that accumulates between frames. If a frame can't be
recomputed from its timestamp, it isn't this style.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **Flat 2D graphic design in motion.** Flat colour, 4–5 tones, no gradients, no photographic
  texture, outlines never thinner than ~3 px at 1080p.
- **Motion is springy and confident.** Entrances overshoot, then settle. Nothing floats in.
- **One accent colour, on one thing.** The accent marks the protagonist; everything else is ground,
  ink and grey.
- **Transitions are carried by an element**, not by a fade: a shape grows to fill the frame and
  *becomes* the next scene's background; an object morphs into its counterpart in the next world.
- **Type is architecture.** One heavy grotesk, extreme scale contrast, a giant number and tiny meta
  text. Captions are drawn whole, never letter by letter.

Not a generative-video look (no film grain, no camera drift, no "cinematic" bokeh as a default), not
a whiteboard explainer (no hand, no pen), not kinetic typography for a lyric video (words don't fly
or bounce), not a 3D render (any depth is layered 2.5D parallax, not perspective).

## 2. The seven canonical looks

Pick one and commit. Mixing them is what makes an amateur "AI animation".

| Look | Essence | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| **Flat vector** | Flat shapes, no outlines or 3 px outlines, 4–5 colours, springy transforms | SaaS promos, onboarding, science explainers |
| **Single line / live drawing** | One continuous line draws at ~800 px/s linear, camera keeps the pen tip in the middle third, one fill moment on a downbeat | Abstract concepts, "how it feels", quotes |
| **Isometric miniature** | True isometric, camera slides never rotates, objects drop 40 px and bounce, three tones per material | Products, systems, "how it works" |
| **Dark tech keynote** | Near-black ground, long-tail eases, one giant number at ~30 % of frame height counting to its exact value, light sweep reveals | AI/dev launches, stats |
| **Watercolour & ink** | Everything is a painted stroke, washes bleed 4–8 px past the ink, paper always visible, linework re-randomises 12×/s from a seed | Human, historical, editorial |
| **Era texture (VHS / CRT / 1920s film)** | One era, committed; seeded per-frame grain, 1 px gate weave, 5 % flicker, 2–3 px chroma shift | Retro, music, nostalgia |
| **Paper collage / mid-century toon** | Cut paper with rough edges, layer-by-layer stop-motion steps, off-register colour a few px off its line, boil on 2s | Friendly, educational, brand-story |

Full palettes, line specs and per-look rendering notes: `references/looks-palettes.md`.

## 3. Timing tables (30 fps)

| Move | Value |
|---|---|
| Overshoot entrance | `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)`, settle within **12 frames** |
| Stagger inside a group | **2–4 frames** (3 is the safe default) — never all at once |
| Shape morph / scene change | **18–30 frames** |
| Keynote move (slow, premium) | `cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1)`, 30–45 frames |
| Line drawing speed | ~800 px/s at 1080p, linear |
| Boil (hand-drawn looks) | redraw every **2 frames** (15 Hz), seeded, so it lives without flickering |
| Drop-in objects | from 40 px above, small bounce, 3-frame stagger |
| Number count-up | ease-out over 20–30 frames, lands on the exact value, then holds |
| Cut rhythm | every shot starts on a beat; key actions land on beats |

Math for the same curves in code: `references/motion-grammar.md`.

## 4. Colour logic

- **A warm paper ground, a warm near-black ink.** Pure `#000` and pure `#FFF` never appear.
- **One accent, one meaning.** If coral means "the thing that matters", nothing else may be coral.
- **One ground colour per scene**, and the accent is never placed on its own colour family.
- Ground rotates between scenes; ink and accent stay fixed for the whole piece.

**Claude-brand flavour** (the look most people mean by "Claude animation"):

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Paper ground | `#F0EEE6` (warm cream) |
| Card / raised surface | `#FAF9F5` |
| Ink | `#191919` |
| Secondary ink / meta text | `#5C5A55` |
| Accent (coral) | `#D97757` |
| Accent dark (pressed) | `#BD5D3A` |
| Cool counterweight | `#6A9BCC` (use at most once) |

## 5. Type

- One family, two weights. A heavy grotesk for titles/numbers, a mono for meta text in the corners.
- **Giant number = ~30 % of frame height.** Extreme scale contrast is the signature: 400 px numeral
  next to 22 px notes.
- Captions: **1–4 words**, one line, drawn whole (`draw each word as one string`, never per letter).
- Set the type on a baseline grid and keep it *outside* the moving layer — the type stays still while
  the world moves.

## 6. Safe area (9:16, 1080×1920)

Keep text and the hero inside **x: 60–1020, y: 250–1600**. Instagram/Reels UI covers the top ~250 px
and the bottom ~320 px; TikTok's right rail eats ~180 px on the right edge at the caption band.

## 7. The deterministic frame contract

If rendering through a browser (best fidelity), the output must be a single self-contained HTML file:

```
- 1080×1920 canvas, CSS-scaled to the window and centred.
- window.seek(t) draws moment t (seconds) immediately, with no side effects.
- Seeded RNG only (mulberry32); no Math.random.
- No CSS transitions, no setTimeout, no accumulation between frames.
- requestAnimationFrame loops seek(t % duration); ?render disables autoplay.
- Fonts: await document.fonts.ready before the first draw.
```

Then capture frame-by-frame (Playwright/Puppeteer screenshots at exact `t`, or HyperFrames /
Remotion) and encode: `-c:v libx264 -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p -r 30`.

## 8. Rendering when there is no browser (this sandbox)

Chromium/Playwright can't be downloaded here (egress is allowlisted to GitHub/PyPI/npm). The style
survives: **port the same easing math to PIL/OpenCV and pipe frames to ffmpeg.** The look comes from
the curves and the palette, not from the renderer.

```python
# 2x supersample -> clean edges -> downscale, the vector-look guarantee
S = 2; W, H = 1080*S, 1920*S
def ease_out_back(t, s=1.70158):           # the overshoot entrance
    t = min(max(t,0),1) - 1; return t*t*((s+1)*t + s) + 1
def ease_out_expo(t):
    t = min(max(t,0),1); return 1 if t>=1 else 1 - 2**(-10*t)
def seg(t0, t1, t):                          # normalised local progress
    return 0.0 if t <= t0 else 1.0 if t >= t1 else (t-t0)/(t1-t0)
```

Ship: `imageio_ffmpeg`'s ffmpeg binary or system ffmpeg,
`-c:v libx264 -crf 20 -pix_fmt yuv420p -r 30 -movflags +faststart`.
A working 6-second reference render lives at `claude-animation-style/build.py` in this repo.

## 9. The 7-part brief template

Copy this, fill the beats, hand it over. It is what makes the output reliable.

```
Make a <N>-second animation in a flat-vector Claude style: clean flat shapes, springy confident
motion, like a modern explainer film. 1080x1920 portrait, 30 fps, loops seamlessly.

STORY (timecoded beats)
- 0.0–1.2 s: <what appears, and how it arrives>
- 1.2–2.6 s: <what changes>
- ...
- <last beat>: <what the loop hands back to the opening frame>

PALETTE
- ground <hex>, ink <hex>, accent <hex> on exactly one element, secondary <hex>.
- No gradients, no pure black, no pure white, outlines >= 3 px.

MOTION
- Entrances overshoot and settle in 12 frames; stagger groups by 3 frames.
- Transitions are carried by a shape that grows into the next background. No fades.
- One slow move; everything else snaps.

TYPE
- Heavy grotesk; one giant number at ~30% of frame height; captions 1–4 words, drawn whole.

IMPLEMENTATION
- Single self-contained HTML file, everything drawn in code, no external images.
- window.seek(t) is a pure function of time; seeded RNG; no Math.random, no timers, no CSS transitions.
- ?render disables autoplay; await document.fonts.ready before the first draw.

OUTPUT
- 1080x1920 MP4, H.264, 30 fps, plus a 6-frame contact sheet with the 9:16 safe area marked.
```

## 10. Frame checklist

Before rendering, look at one still from every beat:

1. Does the frame read in 0.5 s at thumbnail size?
2. Is exactly one thing the accent colour?
3. Is any outline under 3 px at 1080p?
4. Is the giant element big enough to be the signature (≥ 25 % of frame width or height)?
5. Is text inside the safe area, and is it still (not moving) while the world moves?
6. Is there a gradient that should be a flat plane?
7. Does the transition carry an element, or is it a fade?
8. Pure black or pure white anywhere?
9. Do captions exceed 4 words?
10. Does the last frame hand back to the first (loop) or to a clean endcard?

## 11. When NOT to use this style

- Faces, emotion, dialogue, character continuity → needs image generation + our ffmpeg timeline
  pipeline (see `quiet-wolf/`), because code-drawn characters cannot hold a repeatable identity.
- Anything historical or photographic → the style's flatness fights the material.
- Long form (> 60 s) → the look fatigues; change ground colour every ~15 s or switch looks per chapter.
